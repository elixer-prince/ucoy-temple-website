The Sabbath is closed with Havdalah, the blessing over the cup that marks the
Sabbath as ended and the week as begun. It is kept in the home at the end of
Saturday, and at the building whenever the congregation stays together to close
the day.

## Closing Prayer

**(Prayer for Help Against Sin)**

**Leader:** Yahweh, to You be all Honour and Esteem. I beg that a guard could be placed at my mouth, that my lips could be wisely sealed. It would keep me from making mistakes and prevent me from destroying myself with my own tongue!

**Congregation:** O Yahweh my Abba and Master of my life, do not leave me at the pity of my own words; don’t let them cause my downfall. I wish I could be whipped for my thoughts, so that wisdom could discipline my mind. I would not want to be spared when I am wrong; I would not want a single sin to be overlooked! Then I would not keep on sinning, making one mistake after another. I could not fall to my enemies and be humiliated by them.

**Leader:** O Yahweh, my Abba, Master of my life, keep me from being arrogant; protect me from evil desires. Keep me from being overcome by greed or lust! Do not leave me at the pity of these shameless passions, that I may bring honour and esteem to Your name.

## Tehillim (Psalm: 121)

We recite together.

1. I lift my eyes to the hills; Where does my help come from?
2. My help comes from <span dir="auto">יהוה</span>, Maker of the Shamayim (the Heavens) and Erets (Earth).
3. He does not allow my foot to be moved; He who watches over me does not slumber.
4. See, He who is guarding Yisra’Yah neither slumbers nor sleeps.
5. <span dir="auto">יהוה</span> is my guard; <span dir="auto">יהוה</span> is my shade at my right
   hand.
6. The sun does not strike me by yom (day), nor the moon by laylah (night).
7. <span dir="auto">יהוה</span> guards me from all evil; He guards my being.
8. <span dir="auto">יהוה</span> guards my going out and my coming in now and forever.

## Havdalah Ritual

**(The End of Service)**

The blessing over the cup, said once for the words themselves and twice more as
the cup is passed around the table, is the blessing that marks the Sabbath as
ended:

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">

אשר קידשנו במצותיו וצונו להבדיל בין הקדש ובין החול

</div>

The English of the same words:

> As He commanded us and instructed us to distinguish between the sacred and the
> ordinary.

The cup is not emptied until everyone present has drunk from it. Where wine is
not kept, the blessing over the grape juice is said in the same words, and the
closing continues unchanged.

### Opening Section

**Congregation:** Yahweh is my teshuvah (deliverance), I trust and have no fear; Yahweh is my strength and my song, and has become my Yesha.

**Leader:** Yahweh will give strength to his people, Yahweh will baruk his children with shalom (peace). Shalom to him who is far off, and to him who is near, says Yahweh.

**Congregation:** I will exalt the Torah; I will call upon the name of Yahweh.

### Berakah (Blessing) of the Vine

**Leader:** Ba-rukh a-tah Yahweh Abba me-lekh ha-o-lam, bo-rei p’ri ha-ga-fen.

**Congregation:** Praised are You, Yahweh our Father, Sovereign of the universe, who created the fruit of the vine.

### Berakah (Blessing) of the Spices

**Leader:** Ba-rukh a-tah Yahweh Abba me-lekh ha-o-lam, bo-rei mi-nei ve sa-mim.

**Congregation:** Praised are You, Yahweh our Father, Sovereign of the universe, who created various spices.

### Berakah (Blessing) of the Fire

**Leader:** Ba-rukh a-tah Yahweh Abba me-lekh ha-o-lam, bo-rei mo-rei ha-eish.

**Congregation:** Praised are You, Yahweh our Father, Sovereign of the universe, who created the illumination of the fire.

### Closing Berakah

When the cup has been passed, the day is declared closed and the week is
welcomed. The blessing that follows is said aloud, in Hebrew and then in
English:

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">

אשרי אתה ה׳ אלהינו מלך העולם אשר הפריש בין קדש לחול בין אור לחשך בין יום השבת לששת ימי המלאכה

</div>

**Leader:** Ba-rukh a-tah Yahweh Abba me-lekh ha-o-lam palah ba-yin qadosh chol bay-in or cha-shek ba-yin yom-shabat yom-vav a-mal ezer “us” anach-nu palah ba-yin e-meth a-mad he-bel shav agab che-sed to-qeph na-cha-lah tub sha-bu-a amets emun sha-bu-a arukah shalu sha-bu-a bera-kah shalom.

**Congregation:** Praised are You, Yahweh our Father, Sovereign of the universe, who separated between the sacred and the profane, between light and darkness, between the seventh yom (day) and the six yomim (days) of labour. Help us too, we pray, to distinguish between that which is real and enduring, and that which is fleeting and vain. May we value affection and helpfulness more than power and possessions, that the simcha of a tov (good) week, a week of courage and commitment, a week of health and prosperity, a week of berakah and Shalom (Peace). Hallelu’Yah!

The blessing is not said at sunset. It is said after dark, once three stars can
be seen in the sky, because the closing of the Sabbath is a welcome to the
night rather than a farewell to the light. Where the sky stays overcast and no
stars appear, it is said after dark all the same, in the same words. At the
building, the stars are watched for from the doorway before anyone goes in, and
the blessing is said inside once the congregation is gathered.

## Yahweh’s Berakah

**(Aaronic Blessing)**

**Leader:** Yahweh spoke to Mosheh saying speak to Aaron saying speak to the children of Yisra’Yah and this is what you shall say to them.

Yish-va-re-ch’cha Yahweh va’yish’ma’re-cha. Ya-eir Yahweh pa-nav ei-le-cha vi-chu-ne-cha. Yis-sa Yahweh pa-nav ei-le-cha v’ya-seim l’cha shalom.

**Congregation:** Yahweh barak (bless) you and keep you; Yahweh make His face shine upon you and be tov (good) to you; Yahweh lifts His countenance upon you and give you Shalom (Peace).

From this point the Sabbath is over. The words that end it are <span dir="auto">שבת שעברה</span>, the Sabbath has passed, and ordinary work is permitted again from here on. Where a household keeps a custom of singing at the end, the song comes after these words and before any work is done.

[Final blowing of the Shofar]

## Keeping it in a household

The closing is kept the same way in every home, so a guest who is at any table
on a Saturday evening joins in without being asked. The congregation closes the
Sabbath together at the building on the occasions the temple calendar gives, and
those times are in the announcements on the home page and on the
[temple calendar](/).
