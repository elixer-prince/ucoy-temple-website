The Sabbath is closed with Havdalah, the blessing over the cup that marks the
Sabbath as ended and the week as begun. It is kept in the home at the end of
Saturday, and at the building whenever the congregation stays together to close
the day. The order below is the order it is kept in, so you can follow along
from a phone, at the table or in the sanctuary.

## Waiting for the stars

The blessing is not said at sunset. It is said after dark, once three stars can
be seen in the sky, because the closing of the Sabbath is a welcome to the
night rather than a farewell to the light. Where the sky stays overcast and no
stars appear, it is said after dark all the same, in the same words.

At the building, the stars are watched for from the doorway before anyone goes
in, and the blessing is said inside once the congregation is gathered.

## The blessing over the cup

A cup of wine is filled, and the blessing is said over it. Where wine is not
kept, the blessing over the grape juice is said in the same words, and the
closing continues unchanged.

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">

אשר קידשנו במצותיו וצונו להבדיל בין הקדש ובין החול

</div>

The English of the same words:

> As He commanded us and instructed us to distinguish between the sacred and the
> ordinary.

The blessing is said over the cup three times, because it is said once for the
words themselves and twice more as the cup is passed around the table. The cup is
not emptied until everyone present has drunk from it.

## The closing of the Sabbath

When the cup has been passed, the day is declared closed and the week is
welcomed. The blessing that follows is said aloud, in Hebrew and then in
English:

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">

אשרי אתה ה׳ אלהינו מלך העולם אשר קידשנו במצותיו וצונו להבדיל בין קדש החול

</div>

The English of the same words:

> Blessed are You, Lord our God, King of the universe, who has sanctified us in
> His commandments and commanded us to distinguish between the sacred and the
> ordinary.

From this point the Sabbath is over. The words that end it are <span dir="auto">שבת שעברה</span>,
the Sabbath has passed, and ordinary work is permitted again from here on. Where a household
keeps a custom of singing at the end, the song comes after these words and before any work is
done.

## Keeping it in a household

The closing is kept the same way in every home, so a guest who is at any table
on a Saturday evening joins in without being asked. The congregation closes the
Sabbath together at the building on the occasions the temple calendar gives, and
those times are in the announcements on the home page and on the
[temple calendar](/).
