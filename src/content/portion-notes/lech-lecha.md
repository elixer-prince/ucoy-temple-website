## Portion Summary

The third reading from the book of Bereshit is named Lech Lecha (לך לך). It means “go forth.” The first verse says, “Now Yahweh said to Abram, ‘Go forth (lech lecha לך לך) from your country.’” Section Lech Lecha introduces Abraham and tells the story of his pilgrimage in pursuit of Yahweh.

Abram encountered Yahweh, and it changed his life. We have all sorts of different names for this experience. Some call it a conversion. Others refer to it as being "born again." ### Commentary: Torah

#### Ber. (12: 1—9) The call of Abram

Now Yahweh had said to Abram: We know from Acts 7:2-4 that this promise was made to Abram before he left Ur of the Chaldeans. Now that his father was dead and he was compelled to a more complete obedience, Yahweh repeated the promise.

Abram’s partial obedience did not take Yahweh’s promise away. Instead, it meant the promise was on hold until Abram was ready to do what Yahweh said.

Abram would certainly become a giant of amanah (faith), even being the father of the believing (Galatians 3:7); yet he did not start there. We see Abram as an example of growing in amanah and obedience.

Yahweh wanted Abram to leave his country and his relatives, Yahweh promised Abram a land. Specifically, Yahweh promised the land of greater Isra'Yah.

Yahweh promised to make a nation from Abram. He will have enough children and grandchildren and further descendants to populate a nation.

Yahweh promised to barak Abram and to make [his] name great. There is probably no more honored name in history than the name of Abram, who is honored by Jews, Muslims, and Christians.

Yahweh also promised He would barak those who barak you and to curse him who curses you. This remains true today and is a root reason for the decline and death of many empires.

When the Greeks overran Palestine and desecrated the altar in the temple, they were soon conquered by Rome. When Rome killed Sha'ul and many others, and destroyed Jerusalem under Titus, Rome soon fell. Spain was reduced to a fifth-rate nation after the inquisition against the Jews that keep Isra'Yah custom alive; Poland fell after the pogroms; Hitlers Germany went down after its orgies of anti-Semitism; Britain lost her empire when she broke her amanah (faith) with Isra'Yah.)

Not only was Abram promised berakah (blessing), but Yahweh also promised to make him a berakah, even to the point where all the families of the erets (earth) would be barak in Abram.

And the Scripture, foreseeing that Yahweh would justify the Goyim by amanah (faith), preached the tov (good) new to Abraham beforehand, saying, In you all the nations shall be barak. So then those who are of amanah (faith) are barak with believing Abraham. (Galatians 3:8-9)

Abram departed as Yahweh had spoken to him, and Lot went with him. And Abram was seventy-five years old when he departed from Haran. And Lot went with him: This was partial obedience by Abram. Yahweh tsavah (commanded) him to go out from his kindred, and he brought his nephew Lot. Lot would not be a berakah to Abram. He would be nothing but trouble and inconvenience. Once Abram was in the land, Yahweh reminded him of His promise. The land Abram saw belonged to Abram and his descendants.

Abram never owned any of this land except his burial plot (Ber 23:14-20). Yet Yahweh’s promise was enough evidence to assure Abram that he did indeed own the whole country.

And there he built an altar to Yahweh: Abram thought this was important to do. The altar was important to Abram because it was a place to meet with Yahweh, to offer sacrifice for sin, to show submission to Yahweh, and to worship Yahweh.

We also have an altar (Hebrews 13:10). We meet with Yahweh at our own place where we remember the sacrifice Yahshua made for sin (Ephesians 5:2), where we submit to Yahweh as living sacrifices (Romans 12:1), and where we offer the sacrifice of praise (Hebrews 13:15).

He pitched his tent: Even in the land Yahweh gave him, Abram never lived in a house - he lived in a tent. Tents are the home of those who are just passing through and do not put down permanent roots.

A pilgrim is someone who leaves home and travels to a specific destination. A pilgrim has a goal. Abram’s goal was (and our goal) is Yahweh’s city new Jerusalem (Hebrews 11:8-10, 14-16).

#### Ber. (12: 10—20) Abram and Sarai in Mitsrayim

There was a famine in the land: Abram was not wrong for being concerned about famine and feeding his family, but Abram was wrong in thinking Yahweh would not provide for his needs in the place where Yahweh called him to live. After all, Yahweh called Abram to Canaan, not to Mitsrayim. Abram, like most of us, found it easier to trust Yahweh in the far-off promises than in the right-now needs.

When we are tested in this way, we often believe our actions are all right because no harm can come. Though Yahweh barak and protected Abram even in Mitsrayim, he came away with excess baggage and a rebuke from a pagan king. Harm came.

The harm especially shows up later when a slave girl named Hagar - whom Sari received when in Mitsrayim is a source of great trouble to this family.

Please say you are my sister: This was in fact a half-truth. Sarai was Abra'ms half sister (Ber 20:12). Yet a half truth is a whole lie. Abrams intent here was clearly to deceive, and he trusted in his deception to protect him instead of trusting in Yahweh. If you want to do something wrong, you can find some tov reasons to do it. Pharaoh treated Abram well for her sake: Yahweh barak Abram even when he did not do what he should. Yahweh continued to protect Abram, even when Abram acted like a liar. Yahweh did not call back His promise to Abram because the promise depended on Yahweh, not on Abram.

Abram returns to the land promised to him: Even though Abram came back from Mitsayim with great riches, he returned to the same place as before. He was right back where he started. Essentially, Abrams time in Mitsrayim was wasted time. Yahweh could have and would have provided for his needs in Canaan. Even though Yahweh is great enough to bring you even when we disobey, there is still a cost built into disobedience. Abrams unbelief took him from his place of worship; it led him into sin, and caused him to lead others into sin. It made him more confident in his ability to lie than in the protecting power of Yahweh. It even broke apart his family for a while. Finally, even an unrighteous king rightly rebuked him.
Abram also did what he should. Instead of torturing himself about his past sin, he got busy doing what he needed to do: living with the tent as a pilgrim and the altar as a worshipper, calling on the name of Yahweh. He came back to Bethel, back with the tent and the altar, back doing what he should. Yahweh wants us to walk in our first love and our first works (Revelation 2:4-5).
Yahweh Tsavah (commanded) Abram to leave his family behind when he came to the land of Canaan ( Ber12:1), but Abram brought his nephew Lot along with him. Trouble like this was the result. #### Abrams generous offer to Lot.

Since Abram was the eldest, and Yahweh gave all the land to Abram (not to Lot), it was pure generosity on Abrams part that caused him to make this offer to Lot.

Abram was able to fight when the occasion demanded it. He did not yield to Lot out of weakness, but out of love and trust in Yahweh. A few acres of grazing land didn't seem worth fighting for to a man with an eternal perspective.

Abram fulfilled the Re-New Berith principle of love: Let each of you look out not only for his own interests, but also for the interests of others. (Philippians 2:4)

Abram knew he could trust Yahweh. He did it because he learned Yahweh would provide for his needs, and he did not have to worry about being too generous. Abram knew whatever Lot chose Yahweh would make sure Abram prospered.
In Mitsrayim, Abram thought he had to take his miqreh (fate) into his own hands. He had to look out for himself. Now, he was wiser and was willing to let Yahweh look out for his interests. Right or left, it didn't matter to Abram, because Yahweh would be there. Because he trusted in Yahweh, Abram did not have to be obsessed with his own rights and neither do we. The only right we truly have is the right to go to hell. Everything else is the free gift of Yahweh and has nothing to do with our concept of rights.

Lot made his choice purely based on what he could see with his eyes. He cared only for the material abundance of the land, and cared nothing for how it would impact him and his family ruach'ally. As much as anything, amanah (faith) means we do not walk by what we see, but by what we know to be true in Yahweh: For we walk by amanah, not by sight (2 Corinthians 5:7). Abram walked by amanah; Lot walked only by sight.

Pitched his tent even as far as Sodom: It was only this far for now, but later Lot became a leader of this sinful city. Valuing only the things that can be seen will increase his wealth temporarily, but Lot will lose it all.

Of course Lot thought, I can serve Yahweh as well there as here. They probably need a witness. But he deceived himself, The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked; who can know it? (Jeremiah 17:9) It wasnt Lots choice that led his heart astray. His heart was already astray, and it was demonstrated by his choice.

Yahweh confirms His promise to Abram after Lot had separated from him: Yahweh wanted to talk to Abram alone after Lot left. This was a promise made to Abram, not to Abrams nephew.
This promise of the land had been made to Abram when he lived in Ur of the Chaldeans (Ber12:1-3, Acts 7:2-4). Yahweh is now repeating this promise.

## Commentary: Prophets

### Yahweh's People are Comforted

The Haftarah for Lekh Lekha comes from the Nabbi (prophet) Yeshayahu. In the Parashah, it is clear that the youth of Abraham and Sarah is supernaturally restored to them, as they become the conduits of the Promised Seed despite the ravages of old age. Likewise in the Haftarah, those who wait upon Yahweh are said to "renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint."

Rashi writes concerning the "wormlike" quality ascribed to Yacob: "Why was Isra'Yah compared to a worm? To say to you - just as a worm devastates a tree with its mouth, even though it is soft and the tree is hard, so too, Isra'Yah, with the power of prayer, will overcome their enemies who are strong like trees." Other of the Chaz'l (sages) have said that the "worm" here is not a metaphor for Isra'yah but rather a symbol of the ultimate source of despair - death. Yahweh will save His people from even this.

Indeed, Yahshua is the Mashiach by who Yahweh will saves His people Isra'Yah from their sins - and from the ultimate verdict of sin, namely, death. He is the "First and the Last" and there is no other Savior beside Him. All Isra'Yah needs to embrace Him as their long lost Savior.

## Commentary Summary

So Abram said to Lot, "Please let there be no strife between you and me, nor between my herdsmen and your herdsmen, for we are brothers. (Bereshit 13:8)

Abram and his nephew Lot both had herds and flocks. In the land of Canaan, water was limited, and the two kinsmen found it impossible to stay together. Their herdsmen quarreled over grazing territory and water rights.

In this relationship, Abram was Lot's senior. He was clearly in a position of authority over Lot. He could have sent Lot away, sent him back to Haran, or assigned him to any scrap of ground he chose. Instead, Abraham elected to give Lot the first choice of territory and to be content with whatever remained.

Do nothing from selfishness or empty conceit, but with humility of mind regard one another as more important than yourselves; do not merely look out for your own personal interests, but also for the interests of others. (Philippians 2:3–4)

People don't ordinarily behave that way. They usually look out for their own interests, and when a quarrel rears up, they do their best to come out on top. Looking out for "number one" is human nature, but it's not chasid (kind) nature. Yahshua taught an attitude of self-effacement:

Do not resist an evil person; but whoever slaps you on your right cheek, turn the other to him also. If anyone wants to sue you and take your shirt, let him have your coat also. Whoever forces you to go one mile, go with him two. Give to him who asks of you, and do not turn away from him who wants to borrow from you. (Matityahu 5:39–42) By giving Lot the first choice of the land, Abraham exemplified the Ruach of humility and heart of love that the Mashiach described. To Abraham, preserving shalom between brothers was more important than success or prestige. Predictably, Lot chose the best land for himself. Lot was not wicked. He was a normal sort of person, self-serving and looking out for his own interests. Abraham was not normal. "The disciples of Abraham, our father, are generous, poor in Ruach and humble.

Middot U'Mitzvot (Character and Deeds)

Holding Marriage Sacred

Yahweh takes marriage very seriously. In the Scripture, adultery is a sin punishable by death. Today, people change partners and spouses more often than their apartments and houses. Nevertheless, Yahweh has not changed how He feels about marriage. He still holds the marriage union as sacred.

Pharaoh abducted Abraham's wife Sarah for his wife, but he never did get around to consorting with his new bride. Rather than let Sarah's marriage be defiled, Yahweh struck the unsuspecting Pharaoh and his household with a painful, disabling disease. This is a tov example of how something seemingly bad can actually be meant for tov. Pharaoh probably could not see any tov in the fact that he was sick with some awful disease, but that disease spared him from committing a great sin.

Pharaoh probably consulted with his wise men and priests, who identified the phenomenon as some sort of spiritual disfavor. They determined that Sarah, the new addition to the household, must be the source of the problem. They put two and two together and realized she must have been a married woman. Pharaoh's affliction was a punishment for attempting to transgress the bounds of marriage.
