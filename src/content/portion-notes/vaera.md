## Portion Summary

The second reading from the book of Shemoth and fourteenth reading from the Torah is named Va'era ( נ"א ), which means "And I appeared." The title comes from the first words of the second verse of the reading, which says, "And I appeared to Avraham, Yitzchaq, and Ya'aqov, as The Almighty" (Shemoth 6:3). The portion begins with four expressions of redemption whereby Yahweh promises to bring

Yisra'Yah out of the Mitsrayim bondage. The narrative progresses to tell the story of the first seven of the ten plagues that Yahweh unleashed on Mitsrayim.

## Portion Commentary

Deliverance from Bondage

### Thought for the week

Yahweh promised to deliver Yisra'Yah from the burdens of Mitsrayim. The tov (good) news of salvation is that, through amanah (faith) in Yahshua, those burdens can be removed. Instead of sin we have forgiveness. Instead of guilt we have simcah (joy). Instead of condemnation, we have vindication. Instead of death and judgment, we have the gift of eternal life and favor.

### Torah Commentary

#### Shemoth 6:14 | The Genealogy of Mosheh and Aaron

(14-15) The immediate descendants of Reuben and Simeon.

(16-19) The main families descended from Levi.

In the tribe of Levi, there are three main families: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Each of these families will be given specific duties in the service of the Almighty Yahweh and His tabernacle.

(20-27) How Mosheh and Aaron descended from Amram, a ben (son) of Kohath.

This passage not only tells us the ancestors of Mosheh and Aaron, but also some of Aaron's descendants: his bens Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar; and his grand-ben through Eleazar, whose name was Phinehas.

This portion is important because the kohanim (priesthood) that will eventually come forth from the family of Aaron will be passed down to his descendants; so it is important to know exactly who his descendants are.

The bens of Korah (cousins to Mosheh and Aaron; their father Korah was Mosheh' uncle) will also play part in a significant event before Yisra'Yah reaches the Promised Land.

#### Shemoth 6:28 | Mosheh and Aaron Obey Yahweh's Commands

(28-30) Mosheh objects again.

Yahweh had previously given Mosheh a mitzvah (command) to take to the children of Yisra'Yah and to Pharaoh; now Mosheh halts in his obedience.

I am of uncircumcised lips may refer to Mosheh' idea that he has a speech problem; or it may be that he recognizes that he is a sinful man, and therefore unworthy to be used.

Mosheh' feeling may be similar to that of Yeshayahu in (Yeshayahu 6:1-8;) Yeshayahu knew that he was a sinner in Yahweh's presence, and sensed that the "center" of his sin was in his lips - as in speaking and communicating in a way that didn't honor Yahweh. But Yahweh could deal with Yeshayahu's unclean lips, and He is more than able to deal with Mosheh' uncircumcised lips.

And, Yahweh is perfectly able to deal with the things in our life - real or imagined - that hinder us from being used by Him.

Yahweh lays out the plan to Mosheh again.

(1-2) The re-affirmation of Aaron's place in the ministry of Mosheh.

Yahweh is patient with Mosheh; after the outburst at the end of chapter six, we might expect that Yahweh had enough with Mosheh. But Yahweh doesn't even chastise Mosheh; He simply tells him what to do and sets him to do it. Yahweh is rich in! "steadfast love,"

I have made you as Mighty one to Pharaoh: Pharaoh had rejected having any direct dealing with Yahweh (Who is the Almighty, that I should obey His voice to let Yisra'Yah go? [Shemoth 5:2]), so Yahweh would deal with Pharaoh through Mosheh. This would let Mosheh know that when Pharaoh was rejecting him, he was really rejecting Yahweh - and he wouldn't take it so personally!

In the same way, Yahweh will make us "as a Mighty-one" to people we encounter who are rejecting Yahweh; if they harden their hearts or reject us, we shouldn't take it personally!

Aaron your brother shall be your Nabbi: if Mosheh is as Almighty to Pharaoh, then Aaron would be "Almighty's" (that is, Moses') "prophet" - his spokesman before Pharaoh.

Also, even as Mosheh was not to act on his own initiative, but to wait for Yahweh's direction, Aaron was not to act on his own initiative, but to wait for Mosheh' direction.

Yahweh will not allow Mosheh to let the seeming failure of his first encounter with Pharaoh to discourage him; Mosheh is simply commanded, you shall speak all that I command you.

(3) Yahweh promises to harden Pharaoh's heart.

Again, we remember that Yahweh will not harden Pharaoh's heart against Pharaoh's own desire; it is not as if Pharaoh wished to have a tender heart towards Yisra'Yah, but Yahweh would not allow him.

Instead, Pharaoh revealed his heart when he refused the humble request of Mosheh back in 5:1-4; now, Yahweh will merely strengthen Pharaoh in the evil he already has chosen.

Yahweh can do the same toyom; in our rebellion, we may reach the place where Yahweh will strengthen us in the evil we desire: Therefore Yahweh also gave them up to their uncleanness, in the lusts of their hearts... and even as they did not like to retain Yahweh in their knowledge, Yahweh gave them over to a debased mind, to do those things which are not fitting (Romans 1:24, 28).

(4-7) Why Yahweh will harden Pharaoh's heart.

Essentially, it is to bring righteous judgment upon Mitsrayim: so that I may lay My hand on Mitsrayim . . . and the people of Mitsrayim shall know that I am the Almighty. Pharaoh and the people of Mitsrayim said they didn't know who Yahweh was; Yahweh is going to let them know!

Pharaoh displayed the evil in his heart by rejecting a humble request; Yahweh will harden Pharaoh's heart, and Pharaoh will do what he wants to do: sin against Yisra'Yah and Yahweh even more. Then, Yahweh will judge that sin.

#### Shemoth 7:8 | Aaron's Miraculous Rod

Mosheh stands before Pharaoh.

(8-10) Mosheh and Aaron appear before Pharaoh again.

It must have taken real courage to come again, after such disastrous results the first time; but Mosheh is being obedient to Yahweh.

When Yahweh first gave Mosheh the sign in Shemoth 4:1-9, it seemed that the signs were primarily to be for the leaders of Yisra'Yah - but now, Mosheh brings the sign before Pharaoh.

Seemingly, Mosheh did not perform the sign Yahweh had given him back at Mount Sinai the first time he appeared before Pharaoh; now he does, and no doubt he expects it to be very impressive!

(11-13) Pharaoh's magicians imitate the miracle of Aaron's rod.

In the midst of an unmistakable miracle, Ha-Satan has provided Pharaoh with a reason to doubt - and Pharaoh seized on the doubt and hardened his heart.

How did the magicians of Mitsrayim do this with their enchantments? Apparently, this wasn't mere magic - this was demonic power manifested by true supernatural miracles.

Miracles are part of Ha-Satan's arsenal; The coming of the lawless one is according to the working of Ha-Satan, with all power, signs, and lying wonders, and with all unrighteous deception among those who perish, because they did not receive the love of the truth (Torah), that they may be save (2Thessalonians 2:9-10).

Miracles can prove that something is supernatural, but they cannot prove that something is true

These magicians of Mitsrayim were intelligent, learned men; but they lacked the wisdom of Yahweh, as Sha'ul observes concerning them in 2 Timothy 3:7-9: Always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth (Torah). Now as Jannes and Jambres resisted Mosheh, so do these also resist the truth: men of corrupt minds, disapproved concerning the faith; but they will progress no further, for their folly will be manifest to all, as theirs also was.

How could they have not seen the greater power of Yahweh when Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods? These are men who harden their hearts against the evidence, not because of it.

It also shows Yahweh has a sense of humor; what must have been the look on the faces of Pharaoh and the magicians when Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods?

#### Shemoth 7:14 The First Plague: | Water Turned to Blood

(14-18) Yahweh sends Mosheh to warn Pharaoh about the coming of the first plague.

The first plague - as all the plagues - will come because Pharaoh has hardened his heart against Yahweh and His people. in loving-kindness, Yahweh warns Pharaoh, but Pharaoh in his hardness of heart disregards the warning

Yahweh did not plague Mitsrayim because Pharaoh would not let the children of Yisra'Yah go, but because Pharaoh refused to recognize and honor Yahweh: By this you shall know that I am the Almighty

Pharaoh sinned against Yisra'Yah because he was sinning against Yahweh; if he really recognized and honored the Almighty Yahweh of Yisra'Yah, he would free the children of Yisra'Yah. Many of our relationships with people are bad because our relationship with Yahweh is bad.

(19-21) The first plague comes upon Mitsrayim: The Nile turns to blood.

This is the first of the plagues; there are nine in total (the tenth is the slaying of the firstborn, which is in a class by itself), and they are grouped together in threes.

In this structure of threes, the first two plagues only come after warning and a call to repentance; the third plague in each set comes without warning.

Many say this first plague, as well as the other eight, all have naturalistic explanations. When the Nile reaches an extremely high flood stage, it collects finely powdered red earth, and this red earth carry along organisms which color the water and kill fish. But if this were the cause, how possibly could Pharaoh be impressed?

Yahweh may or may not have used natural mechanisms to accomplish these plagues; even if He did, the timing and character of the plagues come from Yahweh's hand alone.

It is important to understand that these plagues were all literal; there is nothing "symbolic" about them. They really happened. This guides our understanding about the plagues in Revelation; there is no reason to see them as "symbolic" either.

The plagues Yahweh brought against Mitsrayim had a definite strategy and purpose; they each confront and attack a prized deity of Mitsrayim. Not only did they bring punishment against Mitsrayim, the plagues also answered Pharaoh's original question: Who is the Almighty, that I should obey His voice to let Yisra'Yah go? (Shemoth 5:2) The plagues show the Almighty Yahweh to be greater than any of the deities of Mitsrayim.
Specifically, this first plague is directed against the numerous river deities of Mitsrayim; the Nile itself was virtually worshiped as a god by the people of Mitsrayim, and Yahweh has shown He has complete power over the river, not some river god.

The god of Mitsrayim Khnum was said to be the guardian of the Nile, and this showed he was unable to protected his territory; the god Hapi was said to be the spirit of the Nile, and was dealt a defeat; the great god Osiris was thought to have the Nile as his bloodstream - now, he is truly bleeding!

In fact, the Nile itself was worshipped as a god.

(22-25) The magicians of Mitsrayim copy the miracle.

How could the magicians of Mitsrayim have fresh water to make as blood, if all the water had been turned to blood? Seemingly, all the waters directly associated with the Nile had been turned to blood (including its pools and tributaries, and water in vessels drawn from the Nile). But water obtained by wells was not plagued: So all the people of Mitsrayim dug all around the river for water to drink, because they could not drink the water of the river. The magicians turned fresh well water into blood.

If the magicians of Mitsrayim really wanted to do a miracle, why didn't they turn the bloody river clean again? Because it seems that Satan cannot perform a constructive, cleansing miracle; he can bring supernatural destruction, but not goodness. All they did was make more bloody water!

One way or another, the result in the heart of Pharaoh is the same - he seizes upon another opportunity to reject and dishonor Yahweh, and Pharaoh's heart grew hard . . . Neither was his heart moved by this.

#### Shemoth 8:1 The Second Plague: | Frogs

The second plague: Frogs.

(1-4) The warning of the second plague.

Yahweh, in His merciful tov'ness, gives Pharaoh another chance at repentance, but Pharaoh will not take it. Why did Yahweh threaten a plague of frogs? The goddess Heqt of Mitsrayim was always pictured with the head of a frog; for this reason, frogs were considered to be sacred and could not be killed. Yahweh will show the people of Mitsrayim the foolishness of a frog-god!

The people of Mitsrayim worshipped the frog as a female goddess because frogs were common around the Nile; the reproduced rapidly; and they were part of two worlds - amphibians, creatures of both land and water.

(5-7) Yahweh brings frogs upon the land through Mosheh and Aaron; the magicians of Mitsrayim do the same. It's hard to miss the humor of Yahweh; He is showing just how ridiculous it is for the people of Mitsrayim to worship a frog-god; it is as if Yahweh is saying, "If you want to worship frogs, I'll give you all the frogs you want!"

"Though Yahweh is the Almighty of hosts he has no need of powerful armies, the ministry of Malaks, or the thunderbolts of justice to punish a sinner or a sinful nation; the frog or the fly in his hands is a sufficient instrument of vengeance.

The ability of the magicians to do the same with their enchantments points to a supernatural power present; this isn't the work of a skilled illusionist, this is occultic power at work.

For all their occultic powers, all the magicians can do is make more frogs! This increases the problem instead of solving it; yet it gives Pharaoh an excuse to harden his heart.

(8-15) Pharaoh asks Moshe for help.

Here, Yahweh's previous promise (Shemoth 7:1) is being fulfilled; Pharaoh will not go to Yahweh himself, but he will go to Mosheh as if he were Almighty.

When Mosheh prays, Yahweh answers - and all the frogs die! This must have been a nauseating scene; the land stank doesn't overstate the reality at all!

Even when Pharaoh's plea was granted, his heart did not change - he hardened his heart - yet Pharaoh did just as Yahweh said he would.

#### Shemoth 8:16 The Third Plague: | Gnats

The third plague: Lice.

(16-17) Yahweh tells Mosheh to initiate the plague of lice.

This plague comes unannounced; Yahweh is not showing Pharaoh the mercy of a warning and an invitation to repentance.

This plague struck at the heart of all the worshipers of Mitsrayim, especially their priests; the priests of Mitsrayim were extremely scrupulous about hygiene and ritual cleansing; an infestation of lice would make them unable to worship their gods.

(18-19) The magicians of Mitsrayim are unable to duplicate this plague.

The plague of lice was also upon every beast; no sacrifices could be made to the gods of Mitsrayim with lice-infesting animals.

If these magicians could use occultic powers to change a rod into a snake, to turn water into blood, and to summon frogs, why could they not bring forth lice? Because as great as Ha-Satan's power is, it is limited - and it comes to its limit rather early.

The testimony of the magicians to Pharaoh (this is the finger of Yahweh) shows they know there is a power greater than theirs, yet a power they do not honor and serve.

The hardness of Pharaoh's heart is shown when he will not even heed the analysis of his own people; there is no rational reason why he insists on resisting Yahweh.

The fourth plague: Flies.

(20-23) Mosheh warns Pharaoh of a plague of flies.

We are not told exactly what Pharaoh's reaction is; but since the plague came, he obviously did not soften his heart towards Yahweh or Yisra'Yah; perhaps the reaction is not described because there was no reaction - perhaps he just ignored Mosheh' message.

To coldly ignore Yahweh is to communicate our hatred of Him as clearly as if we were to try and kill Him; it is a high insult to have Yahweh in your midst and to act as if He doesn't matter.

#### Shemoth 8:20 The Fourth Plague: | Flies

(24) The plague of flies comes.

Literally, it says Yahweh sent a swarm (Hebrew, arowb) upon Mitsrayim; it does not specify what the swarm is of - it may have been a variety of gross insects. Tehillim 78:45 says that these swarms devoured them; there must have been a "biting" element in this swarm.

Yahweh had promised (Shemoth 8:22) that the land of Goshen, the place where the children of Yisra'Yah lived, would be spared this disgusting plague.

(25-27) Pharaoh tries to compromise with Mosheh.

To try and meet halfway - allowing Yisra'Yah a holiday for their Almighty, but demanding they stay within the land of Mitsrayim to worship - is the natural course for a politician, but Yahweh is no politician. He will strike no bargain with Pharaoh.

Mosheh reminds Pharaoh of the social uproar this would cause, and sticks to the original request, refusing to compromise.

We see Yahweh building character and strength in Mosheh; perhaps when he first appeared before Pharaoh, he would have been willing to strike such a bargain. But he knows now that Yahweh is control, and he doesn't have to settle for less than the perfect will of Yahweh.

(28-32) Pharaoh's false repentance.

Intercede for me shows Pharaoh knows exactly who the plagues are coming from, and how they can be stopped (by appealing to Yahweh); yet Pharaoh hardened his heart at this time also.

I will let you go is a clear statement; we cannot tell if Pharaoh deliberately lied to Mosheh or simply changed his mind once the plague of flies was gone.

Many people "turn to Yahweh" in a time of calamity, and when things get better, almost immediately turn their hearts back in hardness to Yahweh. Pharaoh isn't an unusual specimen of humanity; he is a rather typical one.

As we continue in sin, Yahweh can allow our hearts to harden. A man doesn't start by gambling away his paycheck; it starts with continuing on in friendly betting, and his heart grows hard. A man doesn't start with unspeakable perversion; it starts with a few magazines, a couple of videos, and his heart grows hard. A woman doesn't start addicted to alcohol; it starts with some social drinking and her heart grows hard.

#### Shemoth 9:1 The Fifth Plague: | Livestock Diseased

The fifth plague: Disease on livestock.

(1-4) Mosheh warns Pharaoh.

In steadfast love, Yahweh instructs Mosheh to give another warning, so that Pharaoh would have opportunity to repent; but Pharaoh ignores another opportunity.

In this appeal, it is clear that Yahweh wants Pharaoh to let the children of Yisra'Yah go for the sake of Yahweh (Let My people go, that they may serve Me); not even so much for the sake of the children of Yisra'Yah themselves.

We must treat each other well not only for the sake of our fellow brother or sister, but for the sake of Yahweh. We owe it to Him even more than we owe it to them.

(5-7) The death of the diseased livestock.

This plague was directed against Hathor the god of Mitsrayim, who was thought to be a mother goddess was was in the form of a cow; in addition, the religions of Mitsrayim considered cattle sacred and the cow was often a symbol of fertility. Yahweh is showing that He is mightier than this imagined pagan god.

There is a ancient record of a battle That Mitsrayim lost because their enemies put a herd of cattle in front of their advancing troops; the army of Mitsrayim would not shoot at the opposing army for fear of accidentally killing the "sacred" cattle.

Pharaoh was told by Mosheh that the Yisraelites would be spared, and cared enough to confirm this - yet, he did not change his heart when it was proven that Mosheh was exactly right!

#### Shemoth 9:8 The Sixth Plague: | Boils

The sixth plague: Boils.

(8-10) The plague of boils comes without previous announcement.

As the third plague in this second set of three, this plague comes without warning - Yahweh has chosen not to give Pharaoh a previous opportunity to turn.

The idea behind the Hebrew word for boil is "to burn"; it has the idea of a swelling, painful, skin inflammation, and as with some of the previous plagues, this one also affects people and animals.

(11-12) The effect of the boils on the magicians of Midtsrayim.

This plague was probably directed against the god Imhotep of Mitsrayim, who was said to be the god of medicine; even those who would be thought of as closest to the gods of Mitsrayim (the court magicians) were stricken with this plague.

The seventh plague: Hail.

#### Shemoth 9:13 The Seventh Plague: | Thunder and Hail

(13-21) Mosheh warns Pharaoh and the people of Mitsrayim of the plague of hail.

In this extended warning, Yahweh lets Pharaoh know who is in control; as bad as it has been, it has only been by Yahweh's "steadfast love that Pharaoh and Mitsrayim have not been cut off from the earth.

Yahweh goes so far as to demonstrate to Pharaoh that even his resistance is being used for Yahweh's honor; that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth.

If Pharaoh thinks he is accomplishing anything with his resistance against Yahweh, he is dead wrong; all his stubborn rebellion will bring honor to Yahweh name more in the end.

Yahweh invites Pharaoh and the people of Mitsrayim to trust Him by recommending precautions before the plague; some took Yahweh's invitation, but others did not.

(22-26) A plague of hail and lightning, making fire from the shamayim (heavens).

This would have been the most frightening plague thus far; it would have seemed that the wrath of Yahweh was pouring down from shamayim in all severity.

This plague was directed against several gods of Mitsrayim; notable among them would be as Nut, the sky goddess.

(27-35) Pharaoh's false repentance; his heart grows harder.

Pharaoh words of repentance (I have sinned this time. Yahweh is righteous, and my people and I are wicked) sound picture perfect; but this repentance hasn't worked its way into his heart. Pharaoh is grieved at the consequences of sin, but not the sin itself.

Yet, Mosheh is starting to learn, and to discern: I know that you will not yet fear the Almighty Yahweh. Mosheh knew the promise to touch Pharaoh's firstborn (Shemoth 4:22-23) had not yet been fulfilled.

He sinned yet more: hardening our hearts against Yahweh is sin; failure to repent when Yahweh calls is not tov (good).

## Commentary Summary

I will deliver you from their bondage. (Shemoth 6:6)

It is one thing to be burdened. It is even worse to be in bondage. A man might carry his own burden willingly, but it is far more difficult to carry it under the compulsion of another. Yahweh told Mosheh that, in addition to taking Yisra'Yah out from under the burdens of the Mitsrayims, He was going to deliver them from bondage to them.

There are many different types of bondages. Some might find themselves taken into literal bondage like the Jewish and Sabbatarian communities did during World War II or like the persecuted Christians in Sudan and other troubled areas today. Others might find themselves in self-made bondages. It is very easy to give oneself up to financial bondage with things like a high mortgage or other deep debts. We fall into the bondage of our appetites and find ourselves ruthlessly lorded over by addictive behaviors like chemical abuse (drugs, alcohol, nicotine) or even the sins of gluttony and sexual immorality. A person unable to exercise self-control over his own behavior is a person in bondage.

For example, a man says that he is going to quit smoking. The cigarettes are expensive and he knows that it is bad for his health. He tells everyone he is quitting and he tosses his cigarettes into the garbage. A few hours later, he is digging through the trash looking for his cigarettes. He is in bondage to his addiction.

When a person is in bondage, he is not able to liberate himself. He needs someone on the outside to deliver him from the bondage. That is why people who struggle with addictions find their best success when they turn to counseling, support groups and other systems of accountability. It takes someone outside of the situation to intervene.

God is able to intervene and release us from the various bondages that shackle us. Through faith in Yeshua, our chains can fall away.
