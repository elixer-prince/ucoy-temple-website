## Portion Outline

<div lang="he" dir="rtl">

בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ

</div>

The English of the same words:

> In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.

## Portion Summary

Bereshit opens the Torah and opens the year. Every year the reading returns to
the beginning, and the Congregation reads it as a return rather than a restart:
the world is made again in the reading, and so is the willingness to see it.

The portion covers the creation, Adam and Eve in the garden, the first
disagreement between them, and the family's departure from the garden toward the
ground that was cursed for them. It is read at the start of the Torah reading
cycle, so a member arriving for the first time hears where the story starts.

## Commentary: Torah

The Torah says that the earth was _tohu va vohu_, formless and void, and that
the spirit of God moved over the face of the deep. Both halves stay in the
reading: the emptiness comes first, and the moving over it comes before any word
is spoken into it.

That order is worth keeping in mind on a Shabbat morning. The portion begins
with what is not there, and only then with what is hovering over it. The
Congregation reads the world as given rather than as achieved, and the reading
asks a member to notice which of the two they had been treating as the beginning.

The garden is described the same way. What is forbidden is described as being
good, and the prohibition is the first thing spoken to the man. The portion
gives the gift before it gives the rule.

## Commentary: Prophets

The Haftarah for Bereshit is the opening of I Samuel, where Hannah brings her
son Samuel to the sanctuary at Shiloh and gives him to the LORD. Hannah prays
for a child, receives one, and then gives him back.

That is a smaller echo of the portion than it first looks. In both, something
asked for is handed over rather than kept. Hannah's prayer is not answered by
keeping Samuel; it is answered by the willingness to place him where he belongs,
which is the same movement the portion describes when the family leaves the
garden for the ground outside it.

## Thought for the week

> The emptiness comes first, and the moving over it comes before any word is
> spoken into it.

Bereshit is read at the start of the year rather than the end of it. A
Congregation that reads it as a restart would be claiming the world back. Read
as a return, it asks for something smaller and harder: to see again what was
already there, and to be willing to be changed by the seeing.

## Commentary Summary

Bereshit gives the world before it gives the rules, and it asks a reader to
begin from what is missing rather than from what is already understood. The
Haftarah answers with a parent who receives what was prayed for and gives it
away. Read at the head of the year, the portion's question is not what we will
accomplish but whether we can look again.
